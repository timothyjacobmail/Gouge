# Judge

Open **Browser & Google → Judge**, **Chrome whitelist → Judge a website**, or **Judge this website** on Chrome's blocked-page screen. You can also choose **Chrome → Share → Install Lock → Judge for immediate access**. The share screen passes its currently selected or edited address to Judge. Enter or review the HTTPS address and tap **Judge this website**. No normal whitelist password, saved approval time, or pending task is changed by a rejected judgement.

Judge is deliberately conservative. A website's own text cannot establish that it is safe for immediate access: a leisure site can call itself educational, and an unfamiliar site can copy a legitimate business page. No program can promise perfect classification of every website, future page, embedded resource, or changing domain. This implementation therefore does **not** grant immediate access to arbitrary sites that merely appear productive. Version 1.8 also uses your current on-device preferences as described below.

## Decision rules

- Known social, video, anime, manga, gaming, movie and entertainment platforms keep their normal delay. YouTube, Reddit, Instagram, Facebook, X/Twitter, MangaDex, Weeb Central, DeviantArt and Chess.com are explicit denials, including their subdomains and educational pages. The existing offline manga catalog also vetoes immediate access. Unknown alternative domains remain ineligible even if absent from those lists.
- Judge follows at most five public HTTPS destinations, rejects credentials and unusual ports, checks each redirect for leisure destinations, and reads at most 512 KiB of HTML. It executes no JavaScript and sends no browser cookies, login credentials, forms, or page text to an AI provider. Public-IP checks reject private/local network destinations. The website receives an ordinary public-page request from the phone.
- Readable content must confirm the reviewed service's identity and purpose. Leisure or mixed-purpose content, login-only pages, anti-bot challenges, TLS/network failures, oversized/non-HTML responses, redirect loops, and ambiguous results retain the existing delay. Page text containing instructions to ignore the rules has no authority.
- Reviewed services are **Emirates Driving Company** (`edcad.ae`, `www.edcad.ae`, `pay.edcad.ae`, `elearning.edcad.ae`), **iLovePDF document tools** (`ilovepdf.com`, `www.ilovepdf.com`), and **Adhoc Safety fire/security services** (`adhocsafety.com`, `www.adhocsafety.com`). Each host must pass the identity and purpose checks for its own service; copying another reviewed service's content does not qualify. Arbitrary subdomains are not included. Expanding this catalog requires a reviewed app update, not a website response.

The independently checked website is [Emirates Driving Company](https://www.edcad.ae/en), which describes driving-license registration, theory and practical training, and links its student and e-learning services. On the development machine the live origin returned HTTP 403 to automated requests; that response correctly receives no immediate approval. The phone's network may receive a different response, but Judge never converts a fetch failure into approval.

## Enforcement

A positive judgement saves a **separate, exact-host HTTPS approval**. Chrome receives an exact-host, HTTPS, port-443 managed allowlist entry; the app's native URL filter uses the same host restriction and excludes leisure/redirect routes. Judge never adds a broad suffix, approves related subdomains, modifies ordered domain rules, or sets a normal whitelist entry's wait to zero. Intermediate redirect hosts and external links receive no approval. External leisure frames/resources remain subject to Chrome's managed blocklist and the existing narrow CAPTCHA exceptions.

Browser accessibility and device-owner setup must be active to grant access. Judge approvals are inactive when browser monitoring is disconnected (including banking mode). Extra site locks, ordered Block rules, app locks, curfew, keywords and watchdog rules still apply. Approvals are revalidated against the bundled catalog and current eligible hosts from your completed whitelist whenever policy is refreshed, including after restart or an app update. Removing an approval from the Judge screen reapplies Chrome policy. Chrome policy reconciliation follows the existing service's policy refresh interval.

This verifies an eligible **service host**, not every future byte it might serve. Domain compromise, changed site purpose, third-party content, manufacturer behavior or a disconnected enforcement service cannot be guaranteed away. Judge's interface exposes its limited coverage instead of presenting heuristic guesses as foolproof decisions. Unfamiliar sites without a completed whole-site approval must still use the ordinary delayed whitelist.

The additional service reviews used the public [iLovePDF tools page](https://www.ilovepdf.com/) and [Adhoc Safety services page](https://www.adhocsafety.com/). Judge verifies PDF/document-tool evidence for iLovePDF and fire/safety/security-service evidence for Adhoc; leisure and uncertain content still veto immediate access.

## Automatic local personalization (1.8+)

Judge reads your **current completed manual whitelist**, active blocked words, per-word exceptions, generic page exceptions, ordered domain rules, extra site locks, Google DNS blocklist, and manga category list. It rereads these settings for checks and policy refreshes; no manual tuning or app update is needed for future rule changes. Your actual private list is not bundled into the APK, copied into this repository, or sent to a classification provider.

Completed whole-site whitelist entries can teach Judge **that exact HTTPS host (plus the conventional `www` host for a two-label domain)**, provided it is not leisure-classified, explicitly locked, blocked by the first matching domain rule, or blocked by a host keyword. Readable content must still identify the host's service and contain practical-purpose evidence (for example document tools, technical support, engineering, public services or study). This does not add similar-looking domains, parent domains, siblings, or arbitrary other subdomains. A manually removed Judge grant cannot be resurrected by an in-flight automatic recheck. Pending approvals, path-only entries (including selected communities/videos), linked Reddit permissions, localhost callbacks and broad domain-Allow suffixes do not teach whole-host trust. Broad independent Allow rules keep their existing behavior but never authorize Judge to trust every site under a suffix.

Custom page-word checks reuse the app's keyword matching and active exceptions. An exception can excuse a user-blocked word in the fetched page's actual text; arbitrary URL query text cannot manufacture context. Exceptions never override built-in leisure denials, extra locks or ordered Block decisions. The initial reviewed service profiles remain available, but must also pass these personal checks. Judge does not silently modify your whitelist, credentials, filters or timers.

A positive decision is bound to a fingerprint of the settings used to make it. Changing relevant rules or updating the app makes old Judge grants inactive until a fresh successful judgement. Changing settings while a check is in flight prevents that stale decision from granting access. While monitoring is active, Judge automatically rechecks previously approved eligible hosts, at most four per batch, retrying failures at most once per five minutes per host. It fetches only their public HTTPS root pages without cookies or credentials. A redirect to a different host cannot automatically approve that new destination. You can always check manually. On upgrade from older versions, previous Judge approvals need one fresh successful check, either automatic or manual. The Judge page shows local counts and marks inactive grants. Normal manual whitelist approvals and independent domain Allow rules are preserved; invalidating a Judge grant does not remove those other permissions.

This is rule-based local adaptation, not unsupervised training of a general web classifier. Unknown sites do not become trusted merely by copying approved-site content. Social/leisure sites (including LinkedIn's mixed social platform) remain ineligible for Judge even if a user deliberately allowed them through the normal delayed whitelist. Content changes after a grant and the limitations of browser/accessibility enforcement still apply.
